# E2.1 (mini): Do embeddings from a tiny scGPT beat HVG + PCA for cell-type annotation?

- **Roadmap phase**: [Phase 2](../00-plan/roadmap.md#phase-2-single-cell-foundation-models) · Hypothesis ladder rung: 2
- **Status**: done (miniature version; the full E2.1 with released checkpoints is still open)
- **Started / finished**: 2026-11-25 / 2026-11-25
- **Driving paper**: scGPT ([note](../02-paper-notes/scgpt.md)), chosen by citation ranking in `_paper-selection/`
- **Code**: [`scgpt-mini/`](../scgpt-mini/) · results: `scgpt-mini/results/results.json`, `eda.json`

> **Pre-registration.** The Hypothesis, Why, Knowledge, Design and Metric sections were written before the confirmatory 3-seed run. One `--quick` smoke run (1 seed, 3 epochs) happened first. It exposed a binning bug with tied values, which I fixed before the confirmatory run (see Deviations). The model and metric were not tuned on the results.

## Hypothesis

- **H**: a tiny scGPT pretrained with masked expression prediction on pbmc3k gives frozen `<cls>` embeddings that beat HVG + PCA(50) in cell-type macro-F1 under a linear probe. The primary test is the **transfer** setting: pbmc68k, a dataset not used in pretraining. The win must exceed the paired-bootstrap 95% CI in ≥ 2 of 3 seeds.
- **Null**: the scGPT-mini macro-F1 is ≤ PCA's, or the difference CI contains 0. The expected outcome from the critical literature is that PCA is at least as good.

## Why it moves the research forward

The full E2.1 asks this question of real foundation models. Doing it in miniature first teaches me what each part of the pipeline does: the tokenization, the objective, the `<cls>` read-out and the probe protocol. It also builds the evaluation harness (probes, macro-F1, paired bootstrap, random-init and label-shuffle controls). The real checkpoints will plug into the same harness. **(my take)**
- **If H holds** even at toy scale, the cell-language-model inductive bias helps by itself, and the full E2.1 should look at where it helps.
- **If H fails**, I learn what a frozen embedding needs (scale, objective pressure on `<cls>`) before I can trust one. It also reinforces the roadmap rule: always report PCA and linear baselines, and plan to fine-tune rather than use frozen embeddings.

## Knowledge tested

- *Domain*:
  - UMI sparsity and library size.
  - HVG selection.
  - PBMC cell types (T/NK/B/monocyte/DC lineages).
  - Label provenance: clusters derived from expression vs FACS-sorted bulk labels.
  - Abundance imbalance (rare megakaryocytes, DCs).
- *Technical*:
  - Gene-token + value-bin embeddings.
  - Masked value prediction (GEP) and cell-embedding prediction (GEPC/MVC).
  - Frozen-embedding probes (kNN, logistic regression).
  - Macro-F1.
  - Paired bootstrap.
  - Random-init and label-shuffle negative controls.

## Design

- **Data**: scanpy `pbmc3k_processed` (2,638 cells, 8 Louvain-derived labels; `.raw` log-normalized), and `pbmc68k_reduced` (700 cells, 10 FACS-sorted "bulk" labels). Both are restricted to their **764 shared genes**, which are the 68k dataset's HVGs. Both are public 10x data downloaded through scanpy (~30 MB, gitignored).
- **Splits**:
  - Stratified 70/30 split by cell, with seed = pretraining seed.
  - Pretraining uses only the pbmc3k *train* cells.
  - **Limitation: both datasets are single-donor, so a donor split is impossible.** This means in-distribution performance is optimistic for every method.
  - "Transfer" = pbmc68k, a different dataset with different depth and labels from the same tissue. It stands in for an under-covered tissue only weakly.
- **Models**:
  - **scGPT-mini**: d=64, 2 layers, 4 heads, 51 value bins, up to 199 nonzero genes per cell. Trained for 20 epochs, mask ratio 0.4, GEP + GEPC losses.
  - **Baselines**:
    - **HVG + PCA(50)**: genes standardized; fit on the train split.
    - **HVG raw**: the 764 log-normalized genes.
  - **Negative controls**:
    - **random-init**: the same architecture, untrained.
    - **shuffled labels**: probe trained on permuted labels.
- **Probes**:
  - kNN (k=15) on standardized embeddings.
  - Logistic regression (class-balanced).

## Metric and success criterion

Macro-F1 on the test cells. Every class is weighted equally, so rare types count. The criterion is the paired bootstrap of scGPT-mini − PCA (1,000 resamples of test cells, 95% percentile CI). **Primary**: transfer + linear probe, where H requires CI > 0 in ≥ 2/3 seeds. **Sanity checks**:
- Pretraining loss must fall below the "predict the global mean bin" floor.
- Trained must beat random-init. If it does not, the pretraining contributed nothing.
- Shuffled labels must sit near chance.

## Data primer

- **What a row is**: one cell. Its values are counts of mRNA molecules (UMIs) per gene, captured from a droplet. PBMCs are peripheral blood mononuclear cells: T cells (CD4 helper, CD8 cytotoxic), NK cells, B cells, monocytes (CD14+ classical, FCGR3A+ non-classical), dendritic cells and rare megakaryocytes (platelet precursors).
- **Why counts are weird**:
  - Each cell yields only ~2k UMIs out of ~20k genes, so most entries are 0 (dropout plus true absence).
  - The total per cell (library size) varies with capture efficiency, not biology.
  - The standard fix is to normalize to 10k per cell and then log1p.
- **HVGs**: genes whose variance exceeds what their mean predicts. They carry the cell-type signal, and they are the usual input to PCA.
- **The two label sets differ in kind**:
  - pbmc3k labels are *Louvain clusters computed on PCA of these same data*, annotated by marker genes.
  - pbmc68k labels come from *bulk FACS-sorted populations*. They are independent of expression clustering, but noisy: the "Dendritic" class holds 240/700 cells, which is known to be overcalled.

## EDA findings

Figures: `scgpt-mini/results/figures/eda_counts_hvg_bins.png`, `eda_scale_and_labels.png`. Numbers come from `results/eda.json`.

1. **Sparsity is 97.4%** (pbmc3k raw, 2,700 × 32,738), and a median cell detects 817 genes. Within the 764-gene vocabulary, sparsity is 78% (pbmc3k) vs 67% (pbmc68k). → *ML*: scGPT feeds only **nonzero genes** as tokens. Sequence length is a per-cell variable (median 167 vs 243 tokens), and it is a technical covariate the model can latch onto.
2. **Library size**: median 2,197 UMIs, 5–95% range 956–4,221 (4.4×). → *Biology*: depth is technical. *ML*: this is why we normalize, and why scGPT bins values per cell.
3. **70% of nonzero counts are exactly 1 UMI.** → *ML*: per-cell quantile bins collapse. Most quantile edges coincide, so a typical cell has a single huge tied block. Plain `np.digitize` (scGPT's code path) assigns the whole block to the **top** bin. I put ties in the middle of the bins they span. After the fix, the bin histogram is a tall spike from the 1-UMI tie plus a flat tail (panel 4). In sparse data the value token carries much less information than "51 bins" suggests. **(my take)**
4. **Binning removes the dataset scale shift.** The raw log-value distributions of the two datasets differ (`eda_scale_and_labels.png`, left). By construction, the bins are invariant to per-cell rescaling, and this is tested in `test_binning_is_rank_based_and_scale_invariant`. → *ML*: this is the paper's argument for binning: one vocabulary across datasets and technologies.
5. **HVG selection**: only 26% of the 764 shared genes are in pbmc3k's own top-2000 HVGs. → The vocabulary was chosen by the *other* dataset (68k). This handicaps both PCA and scGPT-mini on pbmc3k in the same way, so the comparison stays fair.
6. **Imbalance**: max/min class ratio is 76× in pbmc3k (CD4 T 1,144 vs megakaryocytes 15) and 30× in pbmc68k. → *ML*: accuracy would hide failures on rare types, which is why macro-F1 is the metric and the logistic probe is class-balanced.

## Paper → minimal architecture

| scGPT component | Kept / cut | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Gene-id embedding + value-bin embedding, summed, no positions | **Kept** | This is the core tokenization idea: a cell is a set of (gene, level) tokens |
| Per-cell quantile value binning (51 bins) | **Kept**, with a tie fix | Central to cross-dataset transfer; the fix is needed in sparse data (EDA 3) |
| Nonzero-gene input, subsampled to max length | **Kept** (max 199) | Matches the paper; 199 covers most pbmc3k cells |
| `<cls>` token as cell embedding | **Kept** | This is the object E2.1 evaluates |
| GEP loss (masked value → token output) | **Kept** | The main pretraining signal |
| GEPC/MVC loss (`<cls>` × gene emb → value) | **Kept** | Without it, nothing forces `<cls>` to summarize the cell |
| Generative attention mask (unknown genes attend only to known) | **Cut** | Matters for generation, not for embedding quality; standard bidirectional MLM instead |
| Condition/batch tokens, DAR/ECS integration losses | **Cut** | Single-batch pretraining set |
| 12 layers × 512 dims, ~33M cells, ~1,200 genes | **Cut** to 2 × 64, 1.8k cells, 764 genes | CPU budget under 10 min; this is the biggest caveat on the result |
| Fine-tuning heads | **Cut** | E2.1 is about *frozen* embeddings; fine-tuning is E2.2 |

## Results

3 seeds (0, 1, 2), 20 epochs, CPU, 553 s total. Mean ± sd over seeds:

| Setting / probe | PCA50 | HVG raw | **scGPT-mini** | random init | shuffled labels |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| in-dist (pbmc3k) / kNN | 0.643 ± 0.020 | 0.500 ± 0.009 | 0.266 ± 0.022 | 0.271 ± 0.015 | 0.095 |
| in-dist (pbmc3k) / linear | 0.931 ± 0.017 | 0.929 ± 0.009 | 0.491 ± 0.036 | 0.426 ± 0.023 | 0.087 |
| transfer (pbmc68k) / kNN | 0.316 ± 0.035 | 0.513 ± 0.028 | 0.157 ± 0.005 | 0.122 ± 0.009 | 0.079 |
| **transfer (pbmc68k) / linear (primary)** | **0.692 ± 0.051** | 0.722 ± 0.027 | **0.241 ± 0.039** | 0.212 ± 0.017 | 0.066 |

scGPT-mini − PCA, paired bootstrap 95% CI, primary setting: seed 0 −0.54 [−0.63, −0.41]; seed 1 −0.48 [−0.62, −0.40]; seed 2 −0.33 [−0.44, −0.27]. **PCA wins beyond the CI in 3/3 seeds, in every setting and probe.**

Controls:
- **Pretraining did learn something.** The GEP MSE fell from the mean-bin floor of 0.059 to 0.043 by epoch 19. That is only a 27% reduction, and MVC fell to 0.046 (`pretrain_loss.png`).
- **Trained vs random-init**: trained is better in 3/3 seeds for the linear probe in-dist and for kNN in transfer, but only 2/3 for the primary setting and 1/3 for kNN in-dist. The margins (0.03–0.07) are smaller than the gap to PCA by an order of magnitude.
- **Shuffled labels** sit at chance (0.07–0.10), so the probe pipeline is not leaking.

Figures: `macro_f1.png` and `embeddings_transfer_2d.png`. In the 2-D views, scGPT-mini and random-init split cells into well-separated clusters that **do not follow cell type**. PCA shows a single cloud, with NK and CD34+ separating along one axis.

**Deviations**:
- (1) Tie handling in binning was changed after the smoke run, and documented.
- (2) No donor split was possible, because both datasets are single-donor.
- (3) pbmc3k labels are Louvain clusters on PCA of the same data, which builds in a circular advantage for PCA in the in-dist setting. The transfer setting (FACS labels) does not have this problem, and it is the primary test for that reason.

## Conclusion

- **Verdict**: **H rejected.** It fails decisively at toy scale, in both settings and with both probes. This matches the null expected from the critical literature, but at toy scale it does not test real scGPT.
- **What I learned**:
  1. **Good pretraining loss is not a good embedding.** The model clearly learned to predict values (loss < floor), but `<cls>` barely beats an untrained network. The clusters in the 2-D view suggest that `<cls>` encodes technical structure. My guesses are the number of nonzero tokens, i.e. depth, and the size of the tied 1-UMI block, but I have not checked them. **(my take, untested hypothesis)**
  2. **Random-init transformers are a strong negative control.** Without it, a 0.24 macro-F1 would look like "some signal". Every scFM evaluation should report one.
  3. **Binning is not free in sparse data.** 70% of nonzeros are 1 UMI, so the value channel is mostly a single tied bin. The rank-based input both gives up absolute-level information and depends on how ties are handled.
  4. **Label provenance matters.** On pbmc3k, PCA is graded against labels it helped create. The FACS-label transfer set is the more honest test.
- **Progress signal met?**: partly. Before running, I predicted that PCA would win by a large margin at this scale, and it did. I can't yet predict *where* a real scFM would help.
- **Next step**:
  - E2.1b: regress `<cls>` on n_nonzero and library size to test learning 1.
  - Ablation: GEP-only vs GEP+GEPC.
  - Then the full E2.1 with released scGPT/Geneformer checkpoints, through the same `evaluate.py` harness, on a multi-donor dataset with donor splits.
