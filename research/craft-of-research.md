## Crafting a research topic

- From a broad topic to a focused one
  - e.g. "Free will in Tolstoy" to "The conflict of free will and inevitability in Tolstoy's description of three battles in WAt and Peace"
  - Close to a claim that the reader can find it interesting
- From a focused topic to questions
  - Serious researchers do not document information for its own sake, but to support the answer to a question that they think is worth asking
  - Formulate questions that direct you to just that information you need to answer them
  - Asks "So what?" to find significant questions
    - e.g. "So what, if if I don't understand how the TypeScript compiler works. What will be lost if I don't answer this question?"
    - If the answer is "Nothing", this is not an important question with a significant answer
- Crafting step-by-step
  - Name your topic: "I am trying to learn about \_\_\_\_" (the chosen topic)
  - Add a indirect question: "Because I want o find out who/what/whe/where/whether/why/how \_\_\_\_" (what you don't know about it and why you are pursuing your topic)
  - Answer the question "So what?" by motivating your question: "in order to help my reader understand \_\_\_\_" (why you want your reader to know and care about)
